# Editorial examples

These examples illustrate editorial choices for Ops recaps. Account details and
figures provide context for each example; revalidate them before use in a new deck.

## Compare the right scope

An early Galicia bullet compared total account forecast with a retention-only
target. The corrected version separated the components:

> FY retention forecast: $1.16M against a $1.0M target. Cross-sells built
> jointly with Sales add $231k, for total FY revenue of $1.39M.

This keeps Ops accountability visible and recognizes the joint commercial work.
Similarly, lead the outlook with PS results; label a company-wide Q4 proposal
when it provides useful context.

## Explain the economics

The Q3 draft initially attributed margin improvement to better utilization.
Validation showed utilization had fallen. The final explanation distinguished
higher revenue per reported delivery hour, broadly flat cost per hour, and the
costs between delivery margin and PS gross margin.

The historical bridge was 49.6% delivery margin, less 15.5 percentage points of
allocated costs, yielding 34.1% PS gross margin. Role-level non-delivery time was
part of the explanation, not another cost pool to subtract. Detailed
reconciliations belonged in the notes.

A later pull showed PS gross margin of 34.51%. An editorial pass retained the
earlier snapshot and documented the change instead of mixing the new margin with
the old cost bridge. A requested financial refresh should update the whole bridge.

## Use recognizable language without overstating the result

The user rejected describing Galicia as "a template for system-led expansion."
The revised explanation was more concrete:

> Strong delivery and the right connections put us in place when opportunities
> opened.

Phrases such as "Retention-first is holding" and "the price-cost trap is still
open" linked the recap to the annual plan. Use such language selectively, when
supported by the new period's evidence; do not add a catchphrase to every slide.

## Preserve the strength of a recommendation

"Agree investment limits ... by November" introduced a deadline that the user
later removed. "We propose minimum billable utilization" also overstated the
intended position. The eventual wording was:

> Setting minimum billable utilization thresholds for M2/M3 should probably be a
> priority for FY2027.

For the low-margin accounts, the user preferred first explaining that staffing
was ahead of revenue and that agreed limits or adjustment triggers were missing.
Specificity should clarify the decision without inventing agreement or urgency.

## Keep titles and their evidence connected

"Staffing and pricing decisions are key to improving PS margins" named a topic.
"Allocated costs and account pricing keep PS margin 7pp below target" stated the
finding and set up the margin bridge beneath it.

When the account-achievements title changed to "Strong delivery grew top accounts
and secured key renewals," the internal storyline also needed updating. The user
caught that missed dependency. Review titles, planning text, and supporting claims
together after changing the argument.
